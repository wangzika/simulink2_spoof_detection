\begin{abstract}
GNSS spoofing can bias navigation solutions while preserving plausible receiver outputs.
Fixed-threshold residual monitors are attractive because they are transparent, but they often confuse spoofing with benign degradation caused by low C/N0, poor geometry, RTK ambiguity changes, and LiDAR--inertial alignment transients.
This paper proposes an environment-adaptive sequential generalized likelihood ratio test (EA-SGLRT) that combines raw pseudorange consistency, receiver pseudorange diagnostics, and LiDAR--GNSS residuals under a threshold adapted by C/N0, DOP, satellite count, RTK ratio, and alignment quality.
The detector accumulates multi-cue evidence with a CUSUM-style statistic and outputs confidence and anomaly type.
On a reproducible \MatrixScenarioCount{}-scenario matrix built from a real rover dataset, including clean data, degraded non-attack data, and \MatrixAttackScenarioCount{} synthetic spoofing cases, EA-SGLRT improves mean attack F1 to \AdaptiveSeqFone{} versus \FixedFusedMatrixFone{} for the fixed fused baseline, while reducing mean false alarms from \FixedFusedMatrixFalseAlarm{} to \AdaptiveSeqFalseAlarm{} alarms/min.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
GNSS receivers are vulnerable to spoofing attacks that gradually or abruptly bias the navigation solution.
Measurement-level monitors such as RAIM and innovation tests are deployable without RF front-end modifications, yet their operating point is strongly affected by environmental quality.
Urban canyon multipath, weak signals, poor DOP, and RTK ambiguity changes can produce residuals that resemble spoofing.
This is the central gap addressed here: a detector should become cautious under benign degradation without becoming blind to coordinated spoofing.

We develop a reproducible method and platform for this problem.
The platform extracts RINEX raw observations, parses GPS broadcast ephemerides, computes pseudorange residuals and RAIM statistics, aligns RTKLIB and FAST\_GLIO diagnostics, and evaluates detectors over controlled spoofing and degradation scenarios.
The method, EA-SGLRT, turns these synchronized streams into environment-normalized evidence and applies sequential confirmation.

The contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item an environment-adaptive sequential GLRT that jointly uses raw GNSS residuals, receiver pseudorange diagnostics, and LiDAR--inertial consistency;
    \item a GPS broadcast-ephemeris residual engine with weighted least-squares positioning, RAIM chi-square statistics, and RTK-reference pseudorange residuals;
    \item an observation-level pseudorange attack injector and an \MatrixScenarioCount{}-scenario experiment matrix covering clean, degraded, and synthetic spoofing cases;
    \item reproducible comparisons against RAIM-only, pseudorange-GLRT-only, LiDAR--GNSS-only, fixed fused, adaptive non-sequential, and sensor-ablation baselines.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
GNSS spoofing detection methods include signal-level authentication, multi-antenna angle checks, measurement-level residual tests, navigation-filter innovation monitoring, and cross-modal consistency checks.
Signal-level methods can be powerful but often require receiver internals or specialized hardware.
Measurement-level RAIM and GLRT are transparent and receiver-output compatible, but they are sensitive to geometry and multipath.
Cross-modal methods compare GNSS with inertial, visual, LiDAR, or map constraints, but naive fixed thresholds can overreact to non-attack degradation.
This work focuses on a deployable measurement/navigation-layer detector using RINEX observations~\cite{rinex304}, RTKLIB products~\cite{rtklib}, and LiDAR--inertial diagnostics inspired by FAST-LIO2~\cite{fastlio2}.

\section{System and Features}
Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture} summarizes the pipeline.
RINEX rover observations are decoded into code, carrier, C/N0, LLI, and SSI features.
For GPS satellites, broadcast navigation records are parsed to compute satellite ECEF position, satellite clock correction, elevation, azimuth, and predicted pseudorange.
At each epoch, a weighted least-squares GNSS-only solution estimates receiver position and clock bias; the post-fit residual chi-square forms the RAIM cue.
A second raw cue compares measured pseudoranges with the RTKLIB ECEF reference after estimating a receiver clock offset.

\section{Method}
Let $z_t$ denote the synchronized feature vector at epoch $t$.
EA-SGLRT first computes normalized cue scores: LiDAR--GNSS residual score $S_L$, receiver pseudorange score $S_P$, raw GNSS score $S_R$, Doppler/TDCP score $S_D$ when present, and RTK-quality score $S_Q$.
The raw score is the maximum of the GPS RAIM chi-square ratio and the RTK-reference pseudorange RMS ratio.
The instantaneous fused evidence is
\begin{equation}
G_t=w_L S_L+w_P S_P+w_R S_R+w_D S_D+w_Q S_Q .
\end{equation}

The threshold is adapted with an environment degradation index $E_t$:
\begin{equation}
\tau_t=\tau_0\left(1+\alpha E_t+\beta I_{\mathrm{raw\ missing}}\right),
\end{equation}
where $E_t$ is a weighted combination of low C/N0, high DOP, low satellite count, low RTK ratio, poor RTK status, low healthy-pseudorange count, and rejected pseudorange outliers.
The missing-raw term prevents LiDAR-only excursions from accumulating strong spoofing evidence when broadcast-ephemeris residuals are unavailable.

Sequential confirmation is performed with a CUSUM-style statistic:
\begin{equation}
C_t=\max\left(0,C_{t-1}+\max\left(0,\frac{G_t}{\tau_t}-\eta\right)-\gamma-\lambda E_t\right),
\end{equation}
and spoofing is declared when $C_t>h$.
The detector also reports confidence
\begin{equation}
p_t=\sigma\left(k\left(\frac{C_t}{h}-0.72\right)\right),
\end{equation}
and assigns an anomaly type by the dominant cue: raw-observation outlier, pseudorange bias, LiDAR--GNSS inconsistency, coordinated spoofing, or benign degradation.

\section{Experiments}
The experiment matrix contains clean real data, degraded non-attack data, and synthetic spoofing generated from the synchronized real dataset.
Spoofing cases vary attack strength at 1, 2, 5, and 10 m; ramp time at 1, 5, 20, and 60 s; and attack type across position bias, pseudorange delay, single-satellite outlier, coordinated spoofing, and slow drift.
The degraded non-attack case lowers C/N0, worsens DOP, reduces RTK ratio and satellite availability, and increases moderate multipath-like pseudorange scatter without changing the spoofing label.
This design tests whether a detector separates adversarial consistency violations from ordinary quality degradation.

We compare EA-SGLRT with RAIM-only, pseudorange GLRT-only, LiDAR--GNSS residual-only, fixed-threshold fused detection, adaptive non-sequential fused detection, and ablations that remove raw GNSS, LiDAR--GNSS, environment adaptation, or CUSUM.
For statistical comparison, each synthetic attack scenario is treated as a paired trial between EA-SGLRT and the fixed fused detector.
EA-SGLRT improves F1 by \AdaptiveVsFixedFoneGain{} on average, with bootstrap 95\% confidence interval [\AdaptiveVsFixedCiLow{}, \AdaptiveVsFixedCiHigh{}] and sign-test $p=\AdaptiveVsFixedPvalue{}$.

\section{Discussion}
EA-SGLRT improves over fixed fusion because it changes the decision problem from a single residual threshold to persistent, environment-normalized evidence.
When C/N0 is low, DOP is poor, or RTK ambiguity quality is weak, the detector raises the effective threshold and prevents short LiDAR--GNSS residual bursts from becoming spoofing declarations.
When raw pseudorange residuals and LiDAR--GNSS residuals rise together, evidence accumulates quickly.
The ablation in Fig.~\ref{fig:ablation} shows that raw GNSS and LiDAR consistency are complementary: removing either reduces F1, and removing environment adaptation produces many false alarms in degraded intervals.

\section{Limitations}
The current raw residual engine is GPS-only, and the rover RINEX stream does not expose Doppler observations, so Doppler/TDCP is not yet fully exploited.
The raw residual overlap with FAST\_GLIO is limited to \RawRaimCoverageRows{} synchronized samples; longer synchronized sequences are needed for final statistical claims.
The spoofing experiments are synthetic rather than collected from a live RF spoofing campaign.
They are still useful because the injector modifies observation-level pseudoranges and post-fusion residuals in controlled ways, but a journal submission should validate the final detector on additional open-sky, urban, and real or replayed spoofing datasets.

\section{Conclusion}
This paper converts the previous platform draft into a method-oriented detector: an environment-adaptive sequential GLRT that fuses raw GNSS and LiDAR--inertial consistency.
The implementation is fully reproducible from the repository targets, generates a multi-scenario experiment matrix, and shows improved F1 and reduced false alarms compared with fixed-threshold fusion.
The next research step is multi-constellation raw residual modeling and validation on longer degraded and spoofed field sequences.
