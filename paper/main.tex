\begin{abstract}
GNSS spoofing can bias navigation solutions while preserving plausible receiver outputs.
Fixed-threshold residual monitors are attractive because they are transparent, but they often confuse spoofing with benign degradation caused by low C/N0, poor geometry, RTK ambiguity changes, and LiDAR--inertial alignment transients.
This paper proposes an environment-adaptive sequential generalized likelihood ratio test (EA-SGLRT) that combines raw pseudorange consistency, receiver pseudorange diagnostics, and LiDAR--GNSS residuals under a threshold adapted by C/N0, DOP, satellite count, RTK ratio, and alignment quality.
The detector accumulates multi-cue evidence with a CUSUM-style statistic and outputs confidence and anomaly type.
On a reproducible \MatrixScenarioCount{}-scenario matrix built from a real rover dataset, including clean data, degraded non-attack data, and \MatrixAttackScenarioCount{} synthetic spoofing cases, the selected EA-SGLRT operating point is calibrated from non-attack data under a \AdaptiveSeqFaBudget{} alarms/min budget and reaches precision \AdaptiveSeqPrecision{}, recall \AdaptiveSeqRecall{}, and mean attack F1 \AdaptiveSeqFone{}.
Its main advantage is false-alarm control: it yields \AdaptiveSeqFalseAlarm{} false alarms/min versus \FixedFusedMatrixFalseAlarm{} for fixed fusion and \LioResidualFalseAlarm{} for the high-recall LiDAR--GNSS residual monitor.
The paired comparison with fixed fusion gives mean F1 difference \AdaptiveVsFixedFoneGain{} with bootstrap 95\% confidence interval [\AdaptiveVsFixedCiLow{}, \AdaptiveVsFixedCiHigh{}], quantifying the cost of the conservative operating point.
EA-SGLRT reduces false alarms relative to the LiDAR--GNSS residual monitor by \AdaptiveVsLioFalseAlarmReductionPercent{}\% and forms a \AdaptiveParetoStatus{} point on the F1/false-alarm frontier.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
GNSS receivers are vulnerable to spoofing attacks that gradually or abruptly bias the navigation solution.
Measurement-level monitors such as RAIM and innovation tests are deployable without RF front-end modifications, yet their operating point is strongly affected by environmental quality.
Urban canyon multipath, weak signals, poor DOP, and RTK ambiguity changes can produce residuals that resemble spoofing.
This is the central gap addressed here: a detector should become cautious under benign degradation without becoming blind to coordinated spoofing.

We develop a reproducible method and platform for this problem.
The platform extracts RINEX raw observations, solves rover/base observations with a non-ROS RTKLIB post-processing target, parses GPS/Galileo broadcast ephemerides with an experimental BeiDou path, computes pseudorange residuals and RAIM statistics, aligns RTKLIB and FAST\_GLIO diagnostics, and evaluates detectors over controlled spoofing and degradation scenarios.
The method, EA-SGLRT, turns these synchronized streams into environment-normalized evidence and applies sequential confirmation.

The contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item an environment-adaptive sequential GLRT that jointly uses raw GNSS residuals, receiver pseudorange diagnostics, and LiDAR--inertial consistency;
    \item a multi-constellation broadcast-ephemeris residual engine with per-constellation clock states, weighted least-squares positioning, RAIM chi-square statistics, and RTK-reference pseudorange residuals;
    \item a standalone RTKLIB integration that regenerates the full rover/base post-processed trajectory without ROS and feeds the paper experiments from CMake targets;
    \item an observation-level pseudorange attack injector and an \MatrixScenarioCount{}-scenario experiment matrix covering clean, degraded, and synthetic spoofing cases;
    \item reproducible comparisons against RAIM-only, robust RAIM, EKF innovation, pseudorange-GLRT-only, LiDAR--GNSS-only, fixed fused, fixed CUSUM, adaptive non-sequential, and sensor-ablation baselines.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
The modern spoofing threat was made concrete by portable civilian-spoofer demonstrations~\cite{humphreys2008spoofer}, which motivated receiver-autonomous defenses at several layers.
Signal- and receiver-level monitors examine received power, automatic gain control, C/N0 changes, correlation distortion, or authenticated navigation data.
AGC monitoring can expose overpowering spoofers with minimal computation~\cite{akos2012agc}, and authentication-aided techniques use unpredictable data symbols or partial correlations to detect replay attacks~\cite{seco2021replay}.
More active receiver designs can suppress spoofers in UAV settings~\cite{sathaye2021semperfi}.
These approaches are powerful when receiver internals or authentication signals are available, but they are less convenient for post-processing RINEX/RTK/LiDAR logs or for commodity receivers that expose only navigation and observation products.

Spatial and multi-receiver methods exploit geometry.
Two-antenna differential carrier phase detects spoofing from inconsistencies in the apparent direction of arrival~\cite{psiaki2014twoantenna}.
Crowdsourced double-differenced pseudorange distributions can reveal spatially coherent spoofing over multiple users~\cite{chen2024d2sp}.
Wide-area maritime monitors use AIS-derived motion consistency to detect spoofing or jamming events at scale~\cite{spravil2023nmea,park2026ais}.
These methods provide strong evidence, but they require antenna baselines, cooperative users, or external vessel/traffic infrastructure that is not always available for a single ground robot.

Measurement-level integrity monitoring is closer to the interface used in this paper.
Classical RAIM and GLRT-style residual tests are transparent and receiver-output compatible, and recent work augments RAIM with opportunistic information models~\cite{liu2024gmraim}.
Low-cost COTS monitoring has also shown that commodity receiver outputs can detect jamming and spoofing signatures~\cite{kriezis2025cots}.
However, fixed residual thresholds are sensitive to C/N0, DOP, satellite visibility, multipath, and RTK ambiguity state.
This sensitivity is precisely why a high-recall baseline can suffer low precision under degraded but non-attack conditions.

Cross-modal defenses compare GNSS with inertial, vehicle, or other navigation sensors.
Carrier phase with IMU constraints improves ground-vehicle spoofing detection~\cite{clements2022carrierimu}, consumer INS can be coupled with carrier phase for low-cost detection~\cite{johansson2025consumerins}, and autonomous-vehicle frameworks fuse GNSS with onboard sensors to identify spoofing attacks~\cite{dasgupta2022sensorfusion}.
These works motivate our use of LiDAR--inertial consistency, but many systems still use static decision gates or do not explicitly model benign environmental degradation.
Our contribution is therefore a deployable measurement/navigation-layer detector using RINEX observations~\cite{rinex304}, RTKLIB products~\cite{rtklib}, and LiDAR--inertial diagnostics inspired by FAST-LIO2~\cite{fastlio2}, with an environment-adaptive threshold and sequential GLRT accumulation to separate persistent spoofing evidence from urban-quality degradation.

\section{System and Features}
Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture} summarizes the pipeline.
RINEX rover observations are decoded into code, carrier, C/N0, LLI, and SSI features.
The released experiment target first builds RTKLIB as a standalone C++ library and runs batch post-processing on rover/base observations and the broadcast navigation file.
This produces \RtklibSolutionRows{} RTKLIB solution rows from GPST \RtklibStartStamp{} to \RtklibEndStamp{}, with \RtklibBadRows{} malformed output rows, and removes the earlier dependence on a ROS node or manually exported trajectory.
For GPS and Galileo satellites, broadcast navigation records are parsed to compute satellite ECEF position, satellite clock correction, elevation, azimuth, and predicted pseudorange; BeiDou records are parsed and wired through the multi-clock framework but are kept experimental in the main residual results.
At each epoch, a weighted least-squares GNSS-only solution estimates receiver position and clock bias; the post-fit residual chi-square forms the RAIM cue.
A second raw cue compares measured pseudoranges with the RTKLIB ECEF reference after estimating a receiver clock offset.

\section{Method}
Let $z_t$ denote the synchronized feature vector at epoch $t$.
The null hypothesis $H_0$ contains nominal operation and benign environmental degradation; the alternative $H_1$ contains spoofing that creates persistent inconsistency among raw observations, receiver diagnostics, and LiDAR--inertial odometry.
The key design choice is to avoid treating all residual growth as adversarial.
EA-SGLRT therefore decomposes detection into four steps: raw/cross-modal residual formation, environment normalization, consistency-gated evidence construction, and sequential confirmation.
Fig.~\ref{fig:methodflow} gives the algorithmic flow.

\subsection{Observation and Residual Model}
For satellite $i$ at epoch $t$, the code observation can be written as
\begin{equation}
\rho_{t,i}= \lVert \mathbf{x}_t-\mathbf{s}_{t,i}\rVert
 + c(\delta t_t-\delta t_{t,i}) + b_{\kappa(i)} + I_{t,i}+T_{t,i}
 + \epsilon_{t,i}+a_{t,i},
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{x}_t$ is receiver position, $\mathbf{s}_{t,i}$ is broadcast-ephemeris satellite position, $\delta t_t$ and $\delta t_{t,i}$ are receiver and satellite clock terms, $b_{\kappa(i)}$ is the inter-system clock state for constellation $\kappa(i)$, $I_{t,i}$ and $T_{t,i}$ are ionospheric and tropospheric delays, $\epsilon_{t,i}$ is nominal noise/multipath, and $a_{t,i}$ is a spoofing-induced bias.
Given a weighted least-squares solution or an RTK reference position, the post-fit raw residual is
\begin{equation}
r^R_{t,i}=\rho_{t,i}-\hat{\rho}_{t,i}.
\end{equation}
The RAIM statistic is the weighted squared residual normalized by the chi-square threshold,
\begin{equation}
S_{\chi,t}=
\frac{\sum_i w_{t,i}(r^R_{t,i})^2}
{\chi^2_{1-P_{\mathrm{FA}}}(\nu_t)},
\end{equation}
where $\nu_t$ is the residual degree of freedom after estimating receiver position and clock states.
In parallel, the detector forms an RTK-reference pseudorange RMS score from the same residual set.

The cross-modal LiDAR--GNSS cue uses the position discrepancy
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{e}^L_t=\mathbf{p}^{\mathrm{GNSS}}_t-\mathbf{p}^{\mathrm{LIO}}_t,
\end{equation}
and its Mahalanobis counterpart when covariance or innovation-scale information is available.
Receiver-side pseudorange diagnostics provide RMS and maximum residual statistics from the GNSS update log.
Doppler and TDCP residuals are treated as additional rate-consistency cues when the RINEX observation stream contains the required fields.

\subsection{Cue Normalization and Environment Index}
Each heterogeneous residual is mapped to a dimensionless cue score using robust engineering scales:
\begin{align}
S_L &= \max\left(\frac{\lVert \mathbf{e}^L_t\rVert}{\sigma_L}, \frac{m^L_t}{m_0}\right),\\
S_P &= \max\left(\frac{\mathrm{RMS}^P_t}{\sigma_P}, \frac{\max_i |r^P_{t,i}|}{\sigma_{P,\max}}\right),\\
S_R &= \max\left(S_{\chi,t}, \frac{\mathrm{RMS}^R_t}{\sigma_R}\right),\\
S_D &= \frac{\mathrm{RMS}^D_t}{\sigma_D}, \qquad
S_Q = f(q^{\mathrm{RTK}}_t,\mathrm{ratio}_t).
\end{align}
Here $S_L$, $S_P$, $S_R$, $S_D$, and $S_Q$ correspond to LiDAR--GNSS consistency, receiver pseudorange diagnostics, raw GNSS residuals, Doppler/TDCP consistency, and RTK-quality degradation.

The environment index $E_t\in[0,1]$ summarizes benign conditions that are known to increase residuals:
\begin{equation}
E_t =
\sum_j \lambda_j \,\mathrm{clip}\left(\frac{u_{t,j}-u^{\mathrm{good}}_j}
{u^{\mathrm{bad}}_j-u^{\mathrm{good}}_j},0,1\right),
\end{equation}
where $u_{t,j}$ includes low mean C/N0, low minimum C/N0, high DOP, low satellite count, low RTK ratio, non-fixed RTK status, low healthy-pseudorange count, and rejected pseudorange outliers.
This term does not by itself create an alarm; it only changes the amount of evidence needed to reject $H_0$.

\subsection{Consistency-Gated Evidence}
For fixed-threshold and non-sequential baselines, the instantaneous fused evidence is
\begin{equation}
G_t=w_L S_L+w_P S_P+w_R S_R+w_D S_D+w_Q S_Q .
\end{equation}
The proposed EA-SGLRT uses this additive score only as a diagnostic reference.
Its decision statistic is instead built from agreement among independent cue families.
Let $g_P$, $g_R$, and $g_L$ be weak evidence gates for receiver pseudorange, raw-observation, and LiDAR--GNSS cues.
The observation-level consistency and cross-modal consistency terms are
\begin{align}
B^{PR}_t &= \max\left(0,\min\left(\frac{S_P}{g_P},\frac{S_R}{g_R}\right)-1\right),\\
B^{PL}_t &= \max\left(0,\min\left(\frac{S_P}{g_P},\frac{S_L}{g_L}\right)-1\right),
\end{align}
and a single-satellite consistency term $B^{1s}_t$ is activated only when a large receiver pseudorange excursion is accompanied by raw residual evidence.
The detector also computes a gated weighted score
\begin{equation}
W_t=
0.55[S_P-g_P]_+ + 0.35[S_R-g_R]_+
+0.10\frac{[S_L-g_L]_+}{g_L},
\end{equation}
but only when at least one raw/pseudorange or LiDAR/pseudorange pair passes its gate.
The final consistency evidence is
\begin{align}
\bar{G}_t = \Big[&
\max(B^{PR}_t,0.75B^{PL}_t,B^{1s}_t,W_t) \nonumber\\
&+\omega_D[S_D-g_D]_+ -\lambda_E E_t-\lambda_Q S_Q
\Big]_+ .
\end{align}
This consistency gate is the main change introduced to reduce false positives: degraded but non-attack epochs often excite a single cue family, while spoofing should create persistent agreement among raw observation, receiver diagnostic, and cross-modal residuals.
The environment index $E_t$ and quality cue $S_Q$ reduce evidence rather than directly triggering alarms.
Equivalently, the effective decision requirement increases with benign degradation.
For comparison with non-sequential baselines, the adaptive additive threshold is
\begin{equation}
\tau_t=\tau_0\left(1+\alpha E_t+\beta I_{\mathrm{raw\ missing}}\right),
\end{equation}
where the missing-raw term prevents LiDAR-only excursions from accumulating strong spoofing evidence when broadcast-ephemeris residuals are unavailable.

\subsection{Sequential GLRT Decision}
Sequential confirmation is performed with a CUSUM-style statistic:
\begin{equation}
C_t=\max\left(0,C_{t-1}+\max(0,\bar{G}_t-m-\gamma)-\ell_t\right),
\end{equation}
where $m$ is a consistency margin, $\gamma$ is the CUSUM drift, and $\ell_t$ is a reset leak that increases under benign degradation when no consistency evidence is present.
Spoofing is declared when
\begin{equation}
d_t=\mathbf{1}(C_t>h \;\wedge\; \bar{G}^{\mathrm{raw}}_t>m).
\end{equation}
Here $\bar{G}^{\mathrm{raw}}_t$ denotes the pre-penalty consistency term, so an alarm cannot be caused by environment or quality cues alone.
This rule reduces single-epoch false alarms, but it can delay or miss weak slow-ramp attacks when they do not generate cross-cue agreement.

The detector reports confidence
\begin{equation}
p_t=\sigma\left(k\left(\frac{C_t}{h}-0.72\right)\right),
\end{equation}
and assigns an explanatory anomaly type using the dominant cue pattern.
A high $S_R$ with weak cross-modal evidence is reported as a raw-observation outlier; dominant $S_P$ is reported as pseudorange bias; dominant $S_L$ is reported as LiDAR--GNSS inconsistency; simultaneous large $S_R$, $S_P$, and $S_L$ is reported as coordinated spoofing.
This type is used for diagnosis rather than as a supervised semantic classifier.
Fig.~\ref{fig:cusum} shows the resulting score, adaptive threshold, CUSUM, confidence, and degradation index for a representative attack.

\section{Experiments}
\subsection{Protocol}
The experiment matrix contains clean real data, degraded non-attack data, and synthetic spoofing generated from the synchronized real dataset.
All trajectory references used by the matrix are regenerated by the standalone RTKLIB target rather than copied from ROS runtime output.
Spoofing cases vary attack strength at 1, 2, 5, and 10 m; ramp time at 1, 5, 20, and 60 s; and attack type across position bias, pseudorange delay, single-satellite outlier, coordinated spoofing, and slow drift.
The observation-level injector additionally modifies real per-satellite pseudoranges over the configured +\ObservationAttackStart{}--+\ObservationAttackEnd{} s window, with \ObservationAttackCommonDelay{} m common delay and \ObservationAttackPerSatelliteBias{} m single-satellite bias; in the current file this produces \ObservationAttackRows{} attacked observation rows across \ObservationAttackEpochs{} epochs, with maximum absolute injected bias \ObservationAttackMaxBias{} m after the common-delay term.
The degraded non-attack case lowers C/N0, worsens DOP, reduces RTK ratio and satellite availability, and increases moderate multipath-like pseudorange scatter without changing the spoofing label.
This design tests whether a detector separates adversarial consistency violations from ordinary quality degradation.

We compare EA-SGLRT with RAIM-only, robust RAIM with reference-residual evidence, EKF innovation gating, pseudorange GLRT-only, LiDAR--GNSS residual-only, fixed-threshold fused detection, fixed-threshold CUSUM fusion, adaptive non-sequential fused detection, and ablations that remove raw GNSS, LiDAR--GNSS, environment adaptation, or CUSUM.
Metrics are computed per scenario and then averaged over the \MatrixAttackScenarioCount{} spoofing scenarios; false alarms/min are computed from the clean and degraded non-attack scenarios.
Because a detector can trivially improve recall by alarming too often, we also report the Pareto frontier between mean attack F1 and mean false alarms/min across all baselines and ablations.
The reported EA-SGLRT operating point is selected from the sensitivity grid using only the clean/degraded false-alarm budget: among configurations satisfying \AdaptiveSeqFaBudget{} alarms/min, the most sensitive CUSUM/adaptive-gain pair is used for the attack evaluation.
For statistical comparison, each synthetic attack scenario is treated as a paired trial between EA-SGLRT and the fixed fused detector.
EA-SGLRT changes F1 by \AdaptiveVsFixedFoneGain{} on average relative to the fixed fused detector, with bootstrap 95\% confidence interval [\AdaptiveVsFixedCiLow{}, \AdaptiveVsFixedCiHigh{}] and sign-test $p=\AdaptiveVsFixedPvalue{}$.
Because only one field collection is currently available, we also perform a temporal held-out validation instead of reusing the same samples for both calibration and reporting.
The first \TemporalHoldoutTrainRows{} synchronized rows, up to original time \TemporalHoldoutSplitTime{} s, are used as the calibration segment; the remaining \TemporalHoldoutTestRows{} rows are reserved as an unseen later test segment.
Within each segment, attack windows are re-zeroed to the segment start before synthetic spoofing is applied.
The EA-SGLRT CUSUM threshold and adaptive gain are selected using only clean/degraded non-attack calibration data with a conservative \TemporalHoldoutCalibrationFaLimit{} alarms/min calibration target for a \TemporalHoldoutFinalFaBudget{} alarms/min held-out operating budget.
This is weaker than independent route-held-out validation, but it prevents direct train/test reuse on the current single route.

\section{Discussion}
EA-SGLRT reduces false alarms because it changes the decision problem from a single residual threshold to persistent, environment-normalized consistency evidence.
Urban degradation and spoofing can both increase residuals, but they differ in how evidence is distributed across cues and time.
Benign degradation usually appears together with low C/N0, poor geometry, fewer reliable satellites, and weaker RTK quality; EA-SGLRT treats these as reasons to raise $\tau_t$ and to discount short-lived residual bursts.
Spoofing, in contrast, is modeled as persistent cross-cue inconsistency: raw pseudorange residuals, receiver pseudorange diagnostics, and LiDAR--GNSS residuals should rise together after the ramp.
The present results also show what the method has not yet solved.
The full-data operating point is non-dominated because it satisfies the non-attack false-alarm budget while trading lower false-alarm rate and higher precision for lower recall and F1 relative to fixed fusion; it is not a strict improvement on every axis.
This is consistent with the parameter sweep, where several low-false-alarm points exist but none simultaneously reaches the high recall of the LiDAR--GNSS residual monitor.
The temporal held-out experiment supports this interpretation on unseen later samples from the same collection: the detector stays below the configured false-alarm budget, but recall remains the bottleneck.
The next version should therefore learn or calibrate the environment weights on separate clean/degraded routes, then evaluate on route-held-out spoofing scenarios.

The attack-factor study also clarifies the remaining failure modes.
Slow-drift attacks are hardest because they deliberately keep instantaneous residuals near the benign-motion envelope, which delays CUSUM accumulation.
Single-satellite outliers are easier for pseudorange monitors, so the gain over fixed fusion is smaller than for coordinated or pseudorange-delay attacks.
The explanatory anomaly type is stable in most detected samples, with dominant-cue fraction \AttackClassificationDominantFraction{}, but it should be interpreted as a diagnosis of which cue fired rather than a final semantic attack classifier.

\section{Limitations}
The current raw residual engine uses GPS and Galileo in the main experiments, while BeiDou remains experimental pending GEO/IGSO/MEO-specific corrections and inter-system calibration.
The rover RINEX stream does not expose Doppler observations in the synchronized interval, so Doppler/TDCP is not yet fully exploited.
Although the standalone RTKLIB pipeline now generates a full \RtklibSolutionRows{}-row trajectory and the raw residual engine writes \RawResidualWrittenEpochs{} residual epochs, the main fused evaluation still uses the shorter FAST\_GLIO-synchronized table, where raw RAIM contributes to \RawRaimCoverageRows{} samples.
The full-timeline table and Rerun recording expose the complete RTK/RINEX span, but some LiDAR--GNSS cues are naturally missing after the FAST\_GLIO log ends; longer synchronized sequences are therefore still needed for final statistical claims.
The current false-alarm rate is much lower than the original sequential fusion result, but it remains high for an unattended field-ready spoofing alarm.
The spoofing experiments are synthetic rather than collected from a live RF spoofing campaign.
They are still useful because the injector modifies observation-level pseudoranges and post-fusion residuals in controlled ways while preserving the real trajectory, satellite geometry, C/N0, DOP, RTK status, and LiDAR--inertial logs.
However, a journal submission should validate the final detector on additional open-sky, urban, and real or replayed spoofing datasets, and should report per-route confidence intervals rather than relying on a single synchronized rover sequence.
The current temporal held-out split is useful for the available single route, but it does not remove temporal correlation or site-specific calibration.
The current parameter sweep is therefore a first robustness check; future work should tune on one route and test on unseen routes to remove any remaining dataset-specific calibration.

\section{Conclusion}
This paper converts the previous platform draft into a method-oriented detector: an environment-adaptive sequential GLRT that fuses raw GNSS and LiDAR--inertial consistency.
The implementation is fully reproducible from the repository targets, including a non-ROS RTKLIB post-processing target, raw GPS/Galileo residual generation, multi-scenario spoofing/degradation experiments, and LaTeX figure generation.
The current results show a clear false-alarm reduction relative to fixed fusion and the high-recall LiDAR--GNSS monitor, while also exposing the recall loss caused by strict consistency gating.
The Pareto and sensitivity analyses therefore clarify the remaining research problem: recovering weak-attack recall while keeping the low-false-alarm behavior of the raw/cross-modal consistency detector.
The next research step is BeiDou-specific raw residual calibration, route-held-out validation, and real or replayed spoofing evaluation on longer degraded field sequences.
